\chapter{Introduction}

\guide{Open for a reader who is \emph{not} a specialist. In a few sentences:
what self-supervised pretext tasks are and why they matter for learning visual
representations without labels; then narrow to spatial/position pretext tasks and
name PART~\cite{part} as the immediate starting point. State the one-sentence
gap up front: PART predicts relative \emph{translation} only, and orientation is
left as future work.}

\section{Problem and context}
\guide{Set the scene properly. Contrast \emph{absolute} grid-position pretext tasks
(MAE~\cite{mae}, MP3~\cite{mp3}, DropPos~\cite{droppos}) with PART's
\emph{relative, off-grid} prediction~\cite{part}. Explain, in plain terms, that
PART's target space is the translation subgroup of the plane, and that the natural
unfinished step is the full group of planar rigid motions $\mathrm{SE}(2)$. Mention
that the PART authors themselves flag ``modelling rotations'' as open future work
(their Section~7) and the ``rotated lip implies a rotated nose'' intuition.}

\section{Aims and objectives}
\guide{State the research question crisply: does adding a relative orientation
target $\Delta\varphi$ to each patch pair yield representations that are better on
orientation-sensitive downstream tasks \emph{without} degrading classification?
List 3--4 concrete objectives (design the rotation-aware pretext; implement it
behind a flag; characterise whether $\Delta\varphi$ actually learns; evaluate
downstream vs.\ a translation-only baseline). Keep it tight --- this is a promise
you will measure yourself against in Chapter~4.}

\section{Contributions}
\guide{Bullet the intended deliverables (you can reuse the four from the proposal):
(1)~RoPART, a continuous-rotation pretext $(\Delta x,\Delta y,\Delta\varphi)$
behind a flag; (2)~an intrinsic characterisation of whether $\Delta\varphi$ learns,
with the interpolation confound controlled; (3)~downstream evaluation vs.\ the
translation-only baseline; (4)~a reproducible artefact. Frame the contribution as
\emph{methodological} --- completing the relative pretext task towards rigid motion
--- not a benchmark-chasing exercise.}

\chapter{Background and Literature Review}

\guide{This is the heart of the interim report and the chapter most directly
reusable in the final report. Aim for a \emph{narrative} that converges on PART and
the rotation gap, not a list of paper summaries. Roughly 6--9 pages. Start from
the ``essential'' tier of the background reading.}

\section{Self-supervised representation learning}
\guide{Briefly motivate SSL: learn transferable features from unlabelled images via
a pretext task, then transfer/fine-tune. Place pretext tasks in context and lead
into the spatial family. Keep this short --- it is scene-setting, not the focus.}

\section{Spatial and position-based pretext tasks}
\guide{The lineage that produces PART. Start with relative context prediction
(Doersch et al.~\cite{context2015}) and jigsaw puzzles
(Noroozi \& Favaro~\cite{jigsaw}) as the early patch-spatial pretext tasks; then
the position-prediction / masked-modelling line on transformers: MAE~\cite{mae},
MP3~\cite{mp3}, DropPos~\cite{droppos}. The key axis to draw out: these predict
\emph{absolute} positions or reconstruct masked content on a fixed grid, whereas
PART predicts \emph{relative} geometry between off-grid pairs. This contrast is the
backbone of your argument.}

\section{PART: relative composition of off-grid patches}
\guide{The most important section --- give PART its own detailed treatment, as your
work extends it directly~\cite{part}. Cover: (i)~off-grid continuous patch sampling
(\texttt{v3}) and the absence of position embeddings; (ii)~the pairwise relative
\emph{translation} target $(\Delta x,\Delta y)$; (iii)~the cross-attention relative
encoder trained with MSE; (iv)~the relative-\emph{scale}/aspect extension
$(\Delta w,\Delta h)$ as a proof of concept that the target framework is extensible
(their Table~1), and the reported interference where scale terms delayed
$\Delta x/\Delta y$ convergence; (v)~the negative-symmetry property
$\theta(i\!\to\!j)=-\theta(j\!\to\!i)$ and the patch-uncertainty capability. End by
naming the rotation gap their Section~7 leaves open --- this is your wedge.}

\section{Rotation in self-supervision and in architectures}
\guide{Two distinct ways rotation has entered vision. (1)~As an SSL \emph{objective}:
RotNet~\cite{rotnet} predicts a single global, discrete image rotation --- an
absolute, image-level invariance task, fundamentally different from continuous,
pairwise, relative orientation between parts. Make this distinction explicit; it is
why RoPART is novel. (2)~As an architectural prior: group-equivariant
CNNs~\cite{gcnn}, $\mathrm{E}(2)$-steerable CNNs~\cite{e2cnn}, and spatial
transformer networks~\cite{stn}. Position your project as \emph{learning} relative
orientation as a target, with steerable architecture noted only as a stretch goal.}

\section{Representing and regressing rotation}
\guide{Justify the design choice for $\Delta\varphi$. Direct angle regression is
discontinuous at $\pm\pi$ and a poor neural target; Zhou et al.~\cite{rotcont} show
why continuous representations are needed, motivating a $(\cos\Delta\varphi,
\sin\Delta\varphi)$ target with an angle-aware loss. Note that this also dictates how
intrinsic angular error and the symmetry/consistency metrics must be computed.}

\section{Multi-objective learning and loss balancing}
\guide{Adding $\Delta\varphi$ alongside $(\Delta x,\Delta y)$ (and possibly scale)
is a multi-task regression. Discuss why balancing matters --- PART already reports
inter-channel interference --- and introduce uncertainty-based task
weighting~\cite{kendall} as the principled option to compare against fixed/scheduled
weights. This frames loss weighting as a planned experimental variable, not an
afterthought.}

\section{Orientation-sensitive downstream tasks}
\guide{Set up the evaluation targets. Explain why orientation matters in aerial,
microscopy and document domains, and introduce the oriented-detection benchmark
DOTA~\cite{dota} with an oriented detector such as Oriented R-CNN~\cite{orcnn} as a
realistic application, alongside a lighter relative-pose probe. Be honest about
scope: these motivate the \emph{one} real downstream task you will do properly.}

\section{Notation and preliminaries}
\guide{Optional but recommended (the spec explicitly allows a notation/theory
section). Define the groups you invoke --- translation subgroup, $\mathrm{SE}(2)$,
and $\mathrm{Sim}(2)$ once scale is included --- and fix notation for patch pairs
$(i,j)$, the relative target channels, and the symmetry/compositional-consistency
relations. Keep it to the minimum the later chapters actually use.}

\section{Evaluation and success criteria}
\guide{Define what success looks like --- this is explicitly asked for. Separate
\emph{intrinsic} metrics (angular error on $\Delta\varphi$; rotational negative
symmetry $\Delta\varphi(i\!\to\!j)=-\Delta\varphi(j\!\to\!i)$; compositional
consistency) from \emph{downstream} outcomes (a classification no-degradation check
vs.\ the translation-only baseline; a synthetic orientation probe; one real
orientation-sensitive task). State the comparison baseline and what counts as a
positive result.}

\section{Risks and mitigations}
\guide{Address the validity threats head-on --- markers reward this. The big one is
the \emph{interpolation confound}: rotating and resampling a small patch leaves a
blur signature correlated with $\varphi$, so any positive $\Delta\varphi$ result
needs a matched-interpolation control before it can be claimed as genuine learning.
Also cover orientation recoverability/well-posedness (the signal is sparser than
translation and content-dependent --- potentially turned into an orientation-
uncertainty contribution) and the compute ceiling (full ImageNet-1K ViT-B
pretraining is out of scope; CIFAR-100 / ViT-S is the workhorse).}
